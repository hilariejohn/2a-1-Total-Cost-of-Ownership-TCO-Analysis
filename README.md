# 2a-1-Total-Cost-of-Ownership-TCO-Analysis
- Based on the TCO, which printer is the most cost-effective over 5 years?

Over a 5-year period printing, the Brother Laser Printer is way more cost-effective than the HP DeskJet Inkjet Printer. The laser printer ends up with a TCO of $9,195.20, while the inkjet comes out to a huge $43,037.80. The main reason for this huge difference comes down to the cartridges. The HP inkjet ink cartridges yield around 120 pages for $25 which works out to about $0.21 per page just for ink. Over 195,000 pages, it ends up buying 1,625 ink cartridges, costing $40,625.00. On the other hand, the laser printer uses high-yield toner cartridges that print 3,000 pages each, so you only need 65 toners costing $5,200.00 total. Even though the laser printer uses more electricity ($1,435.20 vs. $7.80), the massive savings on toner make it the obvious choice for heavy printing.
  
- Would the answer change for a home user who prints only 5 pages per week?
  
Yes it would change. If someone only prints 5 pages a week at home, the HP DeskJet Inkjet Printer becomes the cheaper option. At such a low print volume, the inkjet printer's overall cost would only be around $356. Whereas the laser printer would run about $410. When you don't print a lot, the upfront price of the hardware matters way more than the per-page ink costs because you'll barely be buying replacement cartridges.

- What other non-financial factors could influence printer selection?
  
There are a few important qualitative factors to consider such as:
Print Speed (PPM): Laser printers output pages much faster, which is critical in a busy office so people aren't standing around waiting for large documents. 

Print Quality: Inkjets are better if you need to print high-quality photos or color graphics, but laser printers produce much cleaner, sharper text that won't smudge if it gets wet. 

Maintenance & Reliability: If an inkjet sits idle for a while, the liquid ink in the printhead can dry up and clog, forcing you to waste ink running cleaning cycles. Laser toner is dry powder, so it won't dry out even if left unused.   

Size & Power: Inkjets are compact and quiet, while laser printers take up more space on a desk and draw more energy when warming up.

- What cost components are more significant for a large workgroup printer?
  
I would prioritize specs that keep the printer running smoothly with minimal upkeep:  

High-Yield Consumables: Choosing models that support ultra-high-yield toner cartridges and long-life imaging drums so staff don't have to constantly swap them out.   

Paper Capacity & Duplexing: Paper becomes a major expense with heavy use, so automatic double-sided (duplex) printing is a must to save paper costs, alongside large 500+ sheet paper trays to avoid frequent reloads.  

Network Security & Connectivity: Enterprise features like Gigabit Ethernet, Wi-Fi, 802.1X security, and user badge pull-printing to protect sensitive documents. 

Service Warranties (SLAs): Multi-year on-site support contracts so technical issues get fixed quickly without taking down office productivity.

- At what point (in years/pages) do the two printer options break even in cost?
  
The break-even point is the exact number of pages where the toner savings from the laser printer make up for its $155 higher purchase price.   Using the variable ink and toner cost per page:

$$\text{Break-Even Pages} = \frac{\text{Price Difference}}{\text{Per-Page Savings}} = \frac{\$220.00 - \$65.00}{\$0.20833 - \$0.02667} = \frac{\$155.00}{\$0.18166} \approx \mathbf{853 \text{ pages}}$$

This means the laser printer breaks even after printing 853 pages. At a weekly volume of 750 pages, that takes only about 1.1 weeks. Anything printed past 853 pages is pure savings compared to using the inkjet.
